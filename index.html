Lesson Plan 1: Quaid on 11th August, 1947

Reading Comprehension 📅 16-09-2026 Class: First Year (Co-ed 50+)

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • To develop students’ reading comprehension through the prescribed textbook chapter.
  • To improve students’ understanding of vocabulary, phrases, idioms, synonyms, and antonyms.
  • To develop students’ ability to answer comprehension questions and MCQs.
  • To encourage students to identify the main message of the chapter.
  • To promote participation through pair work and whole-class activities.

✨ Learning Outcomes

  • Understand the main ideas of the chapter.
  • Identify and explain unfamiliar vocabulary and expressions.
  • Distinguish between synonyms and antonyms.
  • Answer comprehension questions and MCQs based on the text.
  • Identify the main message of the chapter.

👩‍🏫 Teaching Methods

Reading & Explanation Questioning Pair Work Activity-Based Learning

📝 Teaching / Learning Activities

Reading and explanation of the chapter, followed by word meanings, phrases and idioms, synonyms and antonyms, MCQs, short questions, pair work, and whole-class answer checking.

📊 Assessment Strategies

Oral questioning, MCQs, short questions, vocabulary exercises, and checking students’ answers.

Lesson Plan 2: Parts of Speech through The Word War

Grammar & Morphology 📅 18-09-2026 Class: First Year (Co-ed 50+)

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • To introduce students to the eight parts of speech.
  • To help students recognize parts of speech in a meaningful text.
  • To develop students’ ability to identify examples of different parts of speech.
  • To encourage active participation through an interactive classroom activity.

✨ Learning Outcomes

  • Name the eight parts of speech.
  • Identify different parts of speech in a text.
  • Provide examples of parts of speech from the given story.
  • Apply their understanding through an interactive activity.

👩‍🏫 Teaching Methods

Contextual Teaching Visual Learning Activity-Based Learning Collaborative Learning

📝 Teaching / Learning Activities

Introduction of the Dawn story “The Word War” and explanation of the eight parts of speech using separate charts. Students used sticky notes to find two examples of each part of speech from the story and participated in a boys-versus-girls activity.

📊 Assessment Strategies

Students’ identification of parts of speech from the story and their responses during the competitive activity.

Lesson Plan 3: Word Formation (Prefixes, Suffixes & Roots)

Morphology & Word Building 📅 23-09-2026 Class: First Year (Co-ed 50+)

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • To introduce students to the basic concepts of word formation.
  • To develop students’ ability to identify the components of words.
  • To provide practice in forming words using prefixes and suffixes.
  • To enhance vocabulary building through affixation and root identification.

✨ Learning Outcomes

  • Identify affixes, prefixes, suffixes, roots, and base words.
  • Break words into their meaningful morphological components.
  • Form new words accurately using prefixes and suffixes.
  • Apply newly constructed vocabulary in sentence context.

👩‍🏫 Teaching Methods

Explicit Explanation Guided Practice Written Practice Morphological Analysis

Explanation of affixes, prefixes, suffixes, roots, and base words using board work and contextual examples. Students practised breaking complex words into their core components and formed words using prefixes and suffixes through structured worksheet tasks.

📊 Assessment Strategies

Word-formation exercises, root/affix identification tasks, and worksheet evaluation.

Lesson Plan 4: Self-Introduction as a Student

Speaking & Self-Expression 📅 23-09-2026 Class: First Year (Co-ed 50+)

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • To introduce students to the basic components of a self-introduction.
  • To help students organize personal information in a logical sequence.
  • To develop students’ ability to prepare a short self-introduction in written form.
  • To provide opportunities for students to practice introducing themselves orally.
  • To improve students’ confidence in speaking about themselves.

✨ Learning Outcomes

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

  • Identify the basic components of a self-introduction.
  • Provide personal information in an appropriate sequence.
  • Organize their information into a short paragraph.
  • Deliver a simple self-introduction orally.
  • Speak about themselves with greater confidence.

👩‍🏫 Teaching Methods

Explicit Explanation Guided Practice Written Practice Speaking Practice

📝 Teaching / Learning Activities

The teacher will introduce the basic components of a student self-introduction, including greeting, name, class/grade, school/college, interests or hobbies, future aim, and closing. Students will complete a worksheet by providing their personal information. They will then organize the information into a paragraph and practice delivering their self-introduction orally.

📊 Assessment Strategies

Students will be assessed through their completed worksheets, organization of information into a paragraph, and oral self-introduction practice.

Lesson Plan 5: Lexical Relations: Homonymy, Homonyms, Homophony, & Homophones

Semantics & Vocabulary 📅 25-09-2026 Class: First Year (Co-ed 50+)

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • To check students’ recall of previously taught concepts.
  • To provide students with opportunities to practise self-introduction.
  • To introduce lexical relations in the context of homonyms and homophones.
  • To help students distinguish between homonyms and homophones.
  • To develop students’ ability to apply these concepts in sentences.

✨ Learning Outcomes

  • Recall information from previously taught topics.
  • Give a self-introduction using the previously taught components.
  • Explain the basic concepts of homonymy, homonyms, homophony, and homophones.
  • Identify homonyms and homophones in given examples.
  • Select the appropriate word according to sentence context.

👩‍🏫 Teaching Methods

Questioning Explanation via Examples Guided Practice Competitive Learning

📝 Teaching / Learning Activities

Questions from previous topics were asked to check students’ recall. Students were randomly invited to the dais to give a self-introduction. Lexical relations were then introduced in the context of homonyms and homophones. Examples were provided, followed by identification and sentence-completion activities and a boys-versus-girls speed competition.

📊 Assessment Strategies

Questioning, oral responses, identification activities, sentence completion, and classroom speed competition.

Lesson Plan 6: Punctuation and Capitalization

Mechanics of Writing 📅 30-09-2026 Class: First Year (Co-ed 50+)

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • To develop students’ awareness of the importance of punctuation in written communication.
  • To introduce the appropriate use of common punctuation marks.
  • To improve students’ ability to apply punctuation and capitalization in writing.
  • To develop students’ sentence correction and editing skills.

✨ Learning Outcomes

  • Identify common punctuation marks and their uses.
  • Apply appropriate punctuation to sentences and paragraphs.
  • Use capitalization correctly in written work.
  • Identify and correct punctuation errors.
  • Rewrite a paragraph using appropriate punctuation and capitalization.

👩‍🏫 Teaching Methods

Interactive Storytelling Pair Work Guided Practice Written Practice

📝 Teaching / Learning Activities

The lesson began with an interactive story about a grandmother and a girl. The girl wrote “Let’s eat grandma” without punctuation, which was used to demonstrate how punctuation can change meaning. Students then worked in pairs to add punctuation to given sentences. The main lesson focused on the full stop, question mark, exclamation mark, comma, colon, and semicolon, along with capitalization. Students rewrote a paragraph without punctuation by adding the appropriate punctuation marks. In the second activity, they corrected sentences by fixing punctuation errors.

📊 Assessment Strategies

Punctuation and capitalization exercises, editing, and proofreading written paragraphs.

Lesson Plan 7: Prompt Spark Activity

Spontaneous Speaking & Cognition 📅 02-10-2026 Class: First Year (Co-ed 50+)

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Speak spontaneously on a given topic without lengthy preparation.
  • Organize thoughts within a 30-second time limit.
  • Express ideas clearly, fluently, and confidently at the dais.
  • Develop public speaking and communication skills.
  • Improve quick thinking and cognitive idea generation.
  • Listen to and respond respectfully to peers.
  • Participate actively in a classroom speaking activity.

✨ Learning Outcomes

By the end of the activity, students will be able to:

  • Speak with greater confidence before their classmates.
  • Organize ideas quickly and express themselves clearly.
  • Deliver a spontaneous two-minute speech on an unknown prompt.
  • Demonstrate quick thinking, public speaking, and active listening.

🛠️ Teaching Aids & Materials

Whiteboard, marker, ball, bowl, prompt chits, and timer.

👩‍🏫 Teaching Methods

Spontaneous Speaking Cognitive Prompting Interactive Ball-Passing Constructive Feedback

📝 Teaching / Learning Activities

Introduction: Introduced "Prompt Spark": a prompt provides an idea, while the spark represents cognitive thoughts triggered to connect thinking with spontaneous speaking.

Activity Instructions: Students passed a ball in circles until the teacher clapped. The student holding the ball picked a chit from the bowl, read the prompt, received 30 seconds to organize ideas, and delivered a 2-minute speech at the dais while peers listened respectfully.

Prompts Included: Favorite game; describe yourself in 7–8 lines; favorite teacher; future goals; relaxation activities; strengths & weaknesses; good & bad hobbies; memorable day; ideal personality; 1 million rupees plan; desired skills; most important person; technology usage; best friend; 5-year vision; dream place.

📊 Assessment & Feedback

Assessed on confidence, fluency, clarity of speech, pronunciation, organization of ideas, spontaneous speaking, vocabulary use, and audience interaction. Immediate constructive individual feedback was provided, followed by reflective review of previous internship topics.

Lesson Plan 8: Presentational Skills

Public Speaking & Visual Delivery 📅 07-10-2026 Class: First Year (Co-ed 50+)

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • To introduce the key stages of an effective presentation.
  • To develop students’ ability to plan and organize presentation content.
  • To explain the importance of visual aids and effective delivery.
  • To improve students’ confidence, audience awareness, and question-handling skills.
  • To guide students in preparing for their upcoming presentations.

✨ Learning Outcomes

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

  • Organize presentation content into an introduction, body, and conclusion.
  • Identify suitable visual aids for a presentation (slides and charts).
  • Apply basic presentation and delivery techniques with posture and eye contact.
  • Apply simple techniques to manage nervousness (30-second breathing) and break the ice.
  • Respond appropriately to impromptu and spontaneous questions.
  • Respect speakers and listen attentively while peers present.

👩‍🏫 Teaching Methods

Explicit Explanation Practical Examples Demonstration Interactive Learning

📝 Teaching / Learning Activities

Explained content planning, audience awareness, and presentation structure. Discussed slides and charts as visual aids (using the example of an apple to show how visuals improve retention). Discussed practice, posture, eye contact, time management, formal delivery, reducing fillers, managing nervousness with a 30-second breathing technique, handling spontaneous questions calmly, and audience etiquette.

Assigned Presentation Topics: (1) The Effects of Copy Culture on Education, (2) Artificial Intelligence: Pros and Cons, (3) Corruption in Pakistan: Causes and Effects.

📊 Assessment Strategies

Topic selection readiness, content organization checklists, understanding of visual aids, and formative question-handling practice.

Reflective journal

Weekly reflections on what I learned and what I will change.

Week 1

18-09-2026

What did I learn this week?

I learned about the internship process, required tasks, evidence collection and report preparation, and applied teaching methods and linguistic theories in real classroom settings. I taught a First Year co-educational class of 50+ students through reading comprehension, vocabulary, parts of speech and contextual activities. From the chapter “Quaid on 11th August, 1947” I ran reading and explanation, word meanings, phrases and idioms, synonyms and antonyms, MCQs, short questions, pair work and whole-class checking, and about 85% of students showed good understanding. With the Dawn story “The Word War”, I taught the eight parts of speech using charts and sticky notes in a boys-versus-girls activity, and about 90% identified them correctly. Students understand language better when they work with examples and take part in activities.

What can I do differently next week?

I will focus on individual participation and speaking practice: more chances to come to the dais, use new vocabulary in their own sentences, and complete short independent activities. I will also give extra practice to students who hesitate.

Week 2

25-09-2026

What did I learn this week?

I worked on word formation, self-introduction, lexical relations, homophones and homonyms. I explained affixes, prefixes, suffixes, roots and base words, then introduced the parts of a self-introduction with a worksheet and speaking practice. About 85% of students understood word formation and completed the activities correctly. I invited students to the dais at random to introduce themselves, then taught homophony and homonymy with a boys-versus-girls competition, where about 90% showed good understanding. Written preparation, speaking practice, contextual examples and active participation all matter.

What can I do differently next week?

I will give clearer, better organised instructions before each activity, use short examples for punctuation and multilingualism, check understanding before moving on, and manage activity time so students can think, respond and correct their own mistakes.

Week 3

02-10-2026

What did I learn this week?

I worked on Punctuation, Capitalization, and Presentational speaking skills with young learners through practical classroom activities. The sentence “Let’s eat grandma” demonstrated how lack of punctuation alters meaning. Students worked in pairs, learned the 6 main punctuation marks, rewrote unpunctuated paragraphs, and corrected errors (approx. 85% accuracy). Attended the internship class where Ms. Bakhtawar provided guidance on online portfolio submissions. Later conducted “PROMPT SPARK”, where students picked prompts from chits, organized thoughts in 30 seconds, and spoke at the dais for two minutes. This fostered quick thinking, idea organization, and spontaneous public speaking without lengthy preparation.

What can I do differently next week?

I will focus on strengthening students’ presentational skills by providing more opportunities for them to organize their ideas and present them clearly. I will guide students on structuring content, speaking with confidence, maintaining eye contact, and providing brief preparation time and constructive feedback.

Week 4

09-10-2026

What did I learn this week?

I taught presentational skills, guiding students on content planning, audience awareness, introduction-body-conclusion structure, visual aids (apple demonstration), delivery, eye contact, time management, managing nervousness (30-second breathing), and handling spontaneous questions. Assigned topics: Corruption in Pakistan, Artificial Intelligence, and Copy Culture. In the next session, students delivered presentations applying this framework, used icebreakers, and answered peer questions (approx. 90% applied skills effectively). Followed by teacher feedback and an MCQs assessment activity. Concluded with the final internship class where Ma’am discussed conclusion and submission deadlines.

What can I do differently next week?

I will focus on teaching business letter writing, explaining format, formal language, and practical activities. I will also conduct a comprehensive test covering all internship topics (parts of speech, word formation, self-introduction, homonyms/homophones, punctuation/capitalization, and presentational skills) to evaluate students’ progress and reinforce learning.
